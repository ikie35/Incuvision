--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This document outlines the step-by-step artificial intelligence development
pipeline implemented in IncuVision. The methodology covers five progressive
stages:
  1. Dataset Collection & Assembly
  2. Data Annotation & Cloud Management (Roboflow)
  3. Model Training & Domain Fine-Tuning (Kaggle GPU)
  4. Model Optimization & Format Conversion (ONNX)
  5. Edge System Deployment, Threshold Calibration & Tray Integration (Raspberry Pi 5)

================================================================================
STAGE 1: DATASET COLLECTION
================================================================================
A. Base Multi-Day Candling Dataset (Foundational Knowledge):
   - The initial training dataset consisted of 653 multi-day candled egg images.
   - Images captured eggs at critical developmental milestones across incubation
     (Day 3, Day 7, Day 10, Day 14, and Day 18).
   - Purpose: Expose the neural network to wide biological variances, including
     faint early blood islands, intricate spider-web vascular networks, opaque
     embryo bodies, as well as clear unfertilized yolks and dead-in-shell blood rings.

B. In-Situ Incubator Image Collection (Hardware Adaptation):
   - To adapt the model to the specific physical environment of IncuVision, 20
     high-resolution full-chamber images were captured directly inside the incubator.
   - Setup: Captured by the top-mounted Raspberry Pi camera in a darkened chamber
     with the high-intensity sub-tray LED candling array turned ON.
   - Purpose: Capture the real optical properties of the chamber—including the
     transillumination through the 56-cup yellow turning tray, specific camera
     focal length, viewing angles, and lens vignetting.

================================================================================
STAGE 2: DATA ANNOTATION & PREPROCESSING (ROBOFLOW)
================================================================================
A. Cloud Annotation:
   - The images were uploaded and managed using the Roboflow platform.
   - Every individual egg position in the 56-egg grid was labeled using bounding
     boxes under a binary classification scheme:
     * 'fertile'   : Eggs showing blood vessel branching, embryo eye/shadow, or
                     advanced opacity.
     * 'infertile' : Clear translucent eggs with no vascular development, or
                     floating unfertilized yolk shadows.

B. Preprocessing & Augmentation Strategy:
   - Auto-Orientation: Stripped camera EXIF tags so all images remained upright.
   - Static Resizing: Standardized to 640 x 640 pixels with letterbox padding to
     match YOLOv8's native input aspect ratio without distorting egg curvature.
   - Candling-Safe Augmentation:
     * Flips (horizontal and vertical) and slight rotations (+/- 15 degrees) were
       applied to simulate egg placement and kinematic tray tilting.
     * Controlled brightness variations (+/- 20%) to account for LED power fluctuations.
     * Hue shifting was kept minimal to preserve the true red color of blood vessels.
     * Structural distortions (like Mosaic and Cutout) were turned off to preserve
       the regular geometric layout of the egg tray.

================================================================================
STAGE 3: MODEL TRAINING & FINE-TUNING (KAGGLE GPU)
================================================================================
A. Architecture Selection:
   - Selected Model: YOLOv8 Nano ('yolov8n-detect').
   - Rationale: Lightweight (~3.2 million parameters) with an anchor-free detection
     head, capable of simultaneous localized detection and classification while
     remaining fast enough for low-power edge hardware.

B. Training & Fine-Tuning Execution:
   - Platform: Cloud GPU instance in Kaggle (NVIDIA Tesla T4).
   - Training Workflow:
     1. Initial Run: The model was trained from pre-trained weights on the dataset.
     2. Domain Fine-Tuning: The pre-trained candling weights ('yolov8n_candling.pt')
        were fine-tuned on the in-situ incubator images ('finetune-skerg' dataset).
     3. Backbone Layer Freezing: The first 10 feature-extraction layers were frozen
        (freeze=10) with a low learning rate (lr0=0.0005) to ensure the network
        retained its base blood vessel knowledge while adapting to the incubator.
   - Validation Outcome:
     * The model achieved 81.4% mAP@50 and 78.6% recall on the validation set,
       confirming accurate classification between fertile and infertile eggs.

================================================================================
STAGE 4: MODEL OPTIMIZATION & ONNX CONVERSION
================================================================================
A. Conversion from PyTorch to ONNX:
   - Native PyTorch (.pt) weights require heavy software dependencies that are
     inefficient for real-time edge execution on a Raspberry Pi.
   - The best fine-tuned model checkpoint was exported into Open Neural Network
     Exchange (ONNX) format ('yolov8ncandling.onnx').

B. Export Standards:
   - Fixed static input tensor shape: 1 x 3 x 640 x 640.
   - ONNX Opset 12: Selected as the universal cross-platform standard for full
     compatibility with Raspberry Pi OS (Debian 12 ARM64).
   - Graph Simplification: Simplified the model computation graph to eliminate
     redundant operations, reducing inference latency to ~120 ms per full scan.

================================================================================
STAGE 5: SYSTEM INTEGRATION & REAL-WORLD TUNING (RASPBERRY PI 5)
================================================================================
A. Edge Deployment & Inference Pipeline:
   - The ONNX model was deployed to the Raspberry Pi 5 under ONNX Runtime CPU.
   - Candling Pipeline Workflow:
     1. Automatic Candling Audit (Days 7, 10, 18) or manual user trigger on web app.
     2. The incubator coordinator energizes the candling LED relay.
     3. The camera captures the full chamber frame.
     4. ONNX Runtime runs full-frame detection.
     5. Detected eggs are sorted top-to-bottom, left-to-right into an 8-Row x 7-Column
        grid (#1 to #56) and displayed on the web dashboard.

B. Threshold Calibration for 56-Egg Tray Detection:
   - Initial Issue: The default confidence threshold (0.45) frequently missed 2 to 3
     eggs along the outer edges and corners of the tray.
   - Root Cause: Camera lens vignetting and natural light drop-off made peripheral
     eggs slightly dimmer, causing their raw confidence to score around 0.16–0.22.
   - Resolution:
     * Calibrated CONFIDENCE_THRESHOLD to 0.15.
     * Adjusted IOU_THRESHOLD to 0.60 to prevent tightly packed neighboring eggs
       from canceling each other out during Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS).
     * This ensured reliable detection across all tray positions without false positives.

C. Physical Seating & Optical Insights:
   - Experimental observation showed that placing eggs with the blunt end (air cell)
     facing UP and the narrow end seated directly over the LED aperture provided
     maximum light penetration, ensuring clear egg illumination and high AI accuracy.
